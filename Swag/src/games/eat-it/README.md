# Eat It

Open `/games/eat-it`, choose City or Nature, choose 1–8 participants (turn Bots OFF for solo exploration), then start a bot game or an authenticated multiplayer room. WASD/arrow keys move; dragging creates a floating joystick for touch and mouse. Escape pauses solo play. With Lives enabled, a participant with lives remaining can respawn after five seconds; permanently eliminated participants spectate. F3 shows colliders, direction, velocity, bot state and the radius threshold in development builds only.

## Architecture and reused systems

- The existing React Router tree lazy-loads the game. `src/data/games.ts` supplies the games page, landing cards, navigation, favorites and planets.
- React renders menus, rooms, results and the HUD (10 Hz). Three.js WebGL renders both arenas through `requestAnimationFrame`, using a tilted orthographic camera, directional lighting, shadows and procedural 3D models. Ground textures are drawn locally once per map; labels and the minimap use an overlay canvas. The existing Three.js dependency is reused; no physics package or downloaded asset was added.
- Serializable TypeScript rules run at a fixed 30 Hz in solo play and the Supabase Edge Function. A square-root mass-to-radius function and one Hell scale configuration control logical size. Camera zoom and transforms are smoothed; the rendered mouth radius matches authoritative size exactly.
- Food has planar velocity, friction, pushes and an independent height/vertical velocity for falling and bouncing. Player-to-prop collisions use rotated rectangular footprints, with diagonal circles only for broad-phase checks. Food consumption compares footprint area with mouth area, then checks that the object center is over the visible opening, at lip height or falling, with line-of-sight. Fitting props receive no player-body pushes, so approaching or turning over a snack does not kick it away. Player bites retain their front-entry and inward-motion rules. A claimed object records the direction of lost ground support, tips around the remaining outer lip and accelerates into depth. Models retain their full size. Circular holes discard the ground surface, allowing depth-tested occlusion of the portions below the rim; one reward is granted after the fall. Ordinary food has no remote attraction. Victims are eliminated immediately by the authority and animated locally for 0.56 seconds.
- Spawn management starts with 680 props (hard cap 720) and caps ground power-ups at 18. It cycles map sectors, rejects solid/player/food overlaps and respects the closing boundary.
- Bots use only nearby visible players, food and power-ups. They forage, flee with a sideways escape, hunt, seek power-ups and reposition around obstacles/the closing ring. They receive no mass or movement advantages.
- Supabase auth/profile names, the established Edge Function + optimistic room version + Postgres Realtime pattern, existing friend messages/dialogs, shared language store and shared audio settings are reused. No client-host authority or extra socket service exists.
- Input requests are bounded to one in flight per client, normally every 125 ms, with a 5-second request timeout and cancellation on exit. Clients submit direction, Growth activation, Escape or Respawn intent only. Server-clock fixed steps, normalized inputs, membership checks, shield checks, and compare-and-swap retries protect state. A bounded snapshot buffer interpolates remote transforms 120 ms behind receipt, with at most 160 ms of movement extrapolation. Local movement is predicted for responsiveness; mass, bites, rewards and eliminations are never predicted. Stale input stops after 650 ms and a disconnected human forfeits after 20 seconds. Departed/disconnected hosts transfer ownership. Result recording retains all original humans; the next lobby removes departed seats.
- Lobby reads are idempotent apart from infrequent heartbeats, so receiving a Realtime update does not create an endless write/read cycle. Concurrent joins retry enough times for all eight seats. Navigation or account changes remount the room session instead of carrying an old match into a new route.
- Finished online matches write once into the existing immutable `user_game_results` ledger through a database trigger. The profile's existing aggregate shows Eat It games and wins. Detailed result fields are stored in its `details` JSON. Local bot games are explicitly practice and do not submit unverified profile stats.
- Audio is procedural Web Audio (snacks, bites/chews/gulps, collisions, power-ups, elimination, victory and light City/Nature ambience), using the site's master/effects/music/mute settings. Audio starts after a user gesture.
- All UI strings use the existing translation lookup. Core game labels/results have all seven site languages; additional instructions have English/German, with the existing language fallback for missing entries.

## Balance

All values live in `config.ts`. Starting mass 36 gives radius 24. Radius is `max(14, 4 * sqrt(mass))`, with no upper radius clamp; Hell multiplies the shared body/mouth radius by 2 after the existing survival-size reset. Eating another player requires **1.30× radius**, equivalent to **1.69× mass** before caps/effects, plus front mouth contact, inward motion, physical fit and no shield. Player mass transfer remains 70%.

The mouth diameter is 82% of body diameter. Food fits when its footprint area (`width * height`) is at most the circular mouth area (`π * (0.82 * radius)²`). Length and rotation do not impose additional swallowing limits. Loose props start when their center enters the opening. Fitting large props commit at 25% normalized radial entry, measured across their rotated footprint; outward-moving props cannot commit. Shadows follow the rotated object silhouette and do not contribute to its area. Weight controls inertia; growth is separate metadata. The catalog contains 40+ distinct props across six tiers, including map-specific vehicles and buildings. Rewards soften above 1800 mass. Buildings, vehicles and heavy props tip/fall for 1.15 seconds after valid entry. Small props swallow over 0.48 seconds. Trees descend upright: 0.65 seconds of trunk entry, then canopy contact; a fully fitting tree completes in 1.5 seconds.

Speed lasts 15 seconds (1.5×), Shield 30 seconds and Magnet 15 seconds. Duplicate timed pickups refresh duration without stacking strength. The rare 2x Growth collectible stores one manual activation for a 20-second reward multiplier. `/2` immediately halves future growth for the current life; repeated dividers do not compound. Modifiers multiply, so `/2` with active 2x returns to ordinary rewards. Expiration changes only future rewards, never earned mass. Temporary Size and the generic +30 Growth Boost are removed, including their runtime state and localization. Only Magnet attracts unclaimed eligible props, within its 180-unit extra reach; buildings remain excluded.

City and Nature use different weighted object distributions; Nature adds ground drag. Small props bounce more, large props have heavier inertia, and buildings have no bounce. Sleeping props skip integration. A spatial grid handles awake prop collisions, while the eight-player mouth checks use cheap bounds first. The simulation caps objects at 720, power-ups at 18 and particles at 96. The renderer runs independently from React; the HUD updates at 10 Hz.

Match duration is 2, 10 (default), or 20 minutes. At timeout, remaining contenders enter Hell if enabled, otherwise the established mass-then-score result rules apply, including genuine ties. The old closing-ring behavior remains only for legacy snapshots without a match-duration setting; it cannot prematurely collapse a configured ten/twenty-minute match. A last-survivor victory can finish either mode earlier. The live HUD separately ranks rounded Growth, then remaining lives, with stable seat order for display only.

## Operational limits

The existing backend uses request-driven Edge Functions, not a continuously running simulation process. Rooms advance as connected clients send heartbeats; after an outage, catch-up is bounded to one second and disconnected players forfeit. Every accepted input currently persists a full snapshot. The 8 Hz request rate and Realtime/database bandwidth need hosted two-browser and eight-human load testing before a public launch. This implementation does not deploy remote schema/functions automatically. There is no ranked matchmaking, group-room sharing, or solo cloud history. Planar collisions use simplified footprints; the 3D fall uses a deterministic supported-edge approximation with gravity-like acceleration, rather than a general-purpose rigid-body/contact solver. Area-based eligibility remains unchanged. Some elongated objects can therefore be swallowed even if their flat footprint would not fit a real circular opening. WebGL2 is required. Renderer disposal releases GPU resources on exit. Water remains impassable terrain. The City shrine is protected until its quest is delivered or removed, then enters the physical food catalog; ordinary raised props belong to the finite-mass food catalog.

## Expanded maps and NPC encounters

Both maps now measure **4000 × 3040** (previously 3200 × 2400), approximately 1.58 times the previous playable area. Spawn rejection, the closing boundary, NPC movement, bots, camera clamps, ground plane and minimap use the new bounds. Twelve sectors interleave four weighted districts: City market/park/residential/street and Nature meadow/forest/cabin/rocky areas. Large props receive additional player-spawn clearance. New `smallTree` and `treeTrunk` props bridge progression. Props retain fixed world dimensions after spawning.

Every ordinary prop has finite inverse mass, including buildings. Oversized player contact applies a high effective resistance, capped at a 0.00002 object share of the correction/impulse. The player takes virtually all collision response. Heavy props can still receive impulses from other physical props, integrate motion and fall into a sufficiently large mouth. Sleeping-body optimization and the prop spatial grid remain. Bots test rotated footprints instead of enclosing circles so long props do not falsely close corridors.

`treeGeometry` defines the model's trunk radius (6.5% of width) and horizontal canopy semiaxes (46% of width, 40% of depth). The mouth-fit helper compares these radii with the same circular opening rendered on screen; vertical height and rectangular empty corners do not count as foliage. Trunk fit starts an upright vertical descent. After 0.65 seconds, the canopy reaches the mouth and its width is checked. If it fails, the player is stunned for **3 seconds**, loses voluntary movement and new mouth/quest interactions, but remains vulnerable. The leaves remain stuck above the mouth for those three seconds. A deterministic nearby placement search then sends the intact tree through a 0.85-second reverse-out and tumbling arc; no tree reward is granted. A short reacquisition cooldown prevents immediate repeated capture. Bare tree trunks use ordinary area-based eating. The renderer shows compression, shaking, enlarged distressed eyes and cough marks; the HUD shows a localized countdown.

`quests.ts` owns one serializable encounter per map. The NPC phase is a single state: idle, wandering, flying (pigeon only), running (cat only), swallowing, devoured, emerging, friendly, hostile, leaving or gone. Timers use simulation time. Neutral movement uses seeded destinations, smooth turning and ground obstacle checks. Flying pigeons cannot enter mouths; grounded NPCs require visible mouth overlap and sufficient area capacity. Quest proximity is derived, with 30-unit UI hysteresis, rather than a conflicting NPC phase.

A random City shrine reserves a clear area before props spawn. The **Golden Scroll / Goldene Pergamentrolle** sits beside it. Nature receives a **Cat Tree / Katzenkratzbaum**. Quest items live outside the food/physics arrays and cannot be eaten or magnetized. A thin three-segment arm appears nearby; only the shared hand endpoint touching the item can claim it. Body overlap alone does not. The item then follows the hand outside the body. Only one owner ID exists. Handover requires hand proximity to a neutral NPC and immediately records one completion.

Friendly NPCs follow the recipient for **60 seconds**. Every **4 seconds**, they carry/toss an apple over 0.65 seconds from their actual position toward the mouth, then send it with **3× normal growth** into the existing airborne → mouth-entry → falling → consumption flow (fourteen possible feeds before expiry, hard food cap respected). These apples are reserved to the recipient; missed food remains available to that player. The bonus is attached only to food earned during the friendly interval. The visible apple arc connects the NPC to the reward. No size is added per animation frame.

Eating a neutral NPC uses a 0.48-second swallow and grants a small one-time snack reward (8 pigeon / 12 cat base mass), then hides the NPC for **2 seconds**. It re-emerges for 0.65 seconds: pigeon flies upward, cat jumps. The larger red-eyed hostile form pursues for **20 seconds**, attempting a hit at **5, 10, 15 and 20 seconds**. Hits require face proximity and stun for **1 second**, without reducing mass. Pigeons dive, cats briefly jump, and both retreat between attacks. Squashed eyes, a shaking rim, stars and scratch marks communicate the brief injury. Ground obstacles can let players evade the cat. Both NPCs enter leaving, then the terminal gone state with no target or pending action timer.

Eliminating/disconnecting a carrier returns the undelivered item to its reserved original location. NPC target elimination cancels its encounter reward/revenge. Swallowing the neutral NPC intentionally retires its quest item, including any carried copy, because that NPC no longer accepts a handover. Match completion retires items and NPC activity immediately. Already earned food becomes ordinary food if its recipient is eliminated. Reconnecting clients receive the current authoritative state; they do not restart timers or rewards. Older running snapshots without an encounter continue safely; start a new match to obtain an NPC.

`QuestVisuals` adds pooled procedural NPC, item, shrine and arm meshes to the existing Three.js scene. Shared `eyeProportion` decreases gradually from about 19% of body radius for starters to about 11% for large monsters; whites, pupils and highlights remain visible while the mouth keeps its 82% radius. Both human and bot models use it. React still only updates the HUD at 10 Hz. English, German, Spanish and Portuguese cover all added player-facing text.

NPC position/phase, spawns, ownership, completion, reward food, stun and cleanup run in `stepGame` on the same Edge Function authority. Existing room version comparisons protect concurrent updates. Snapshot interpolation smooths only NPC transforms; it cannot apply an attack or a reward. Deploy the updated `eat-it-match` function together with the frontend; no new table or migration is required.

For repeatable visual checks, open `/tests/eat-it-quests-preview.html` in the Vite development server. It exposes both maps and World, Quest item, Carrying, Friendly, Revenge, Choking and Oversized scenarios with eight participants. This is a development fixture, not a production route. See `EAT_IT_ENCOUNTERS_REPORT.md` for final verification and remaining operational limits.

## Lives, Pluto, Escape and Hell

The existing simulation and Three.js canvas renderer now also support:

- **Singleplayer:** three lives per human/bot. A death spends one life. After five simulation seconds, humans choose Respawn and bots return automatically. Resetting clears every timed effect, choking, claims, carried quests and helper/ride state. All use the same safe spawn rejection. The normal countdown defaults to ten minutes (with two- and twenty-minute options); eligible participants (including waiting participants with a remaining life) transition together to Hell. Normal-map mass is recorded and Hell bodies reset to starting size.
- **Multiplayer:** Animals, Hell and Lives default to enabled, using host-created room settings. Three lives use authoritative respawn requests; Lives OFF gives everyone one life. Bot difficulty selection only changes solo practice. Only the authority accepts Escape/Respawn intent, picks destinations, applies rewards, advances cooldowns, destroys ground and resolves results. Reconnecting uses the stored state. Old room settings/snapshots continue with their original rules.
- **Underpasses:** vehicle and raised-building metadata separates visual and physical footprints, clearance, devour area and render layer. Players retain underpass access until their mouth reaches the actual devour threshold, without wheel/stilt or height-based blocking. Mouth-fit consumption takes priority once large enough. The actual elevated meshes and depth buffer occlude characters; no 2D sorting trick is used. The shrine, logs, trees and solid terrain do not gain underpasses.
- **Pluto:** five ordinary consumables, with 55/25/12/6/2 weighted sizes. Six initial spawn attempts, a twelve-second spawn interval and a twelve-active cap keep them uncommon. Existing geometry/spawn rejection also protects spawn locations. Their normal mouth-entry and fall determine ownership and reward. `EAT.pluto` supplies the default **2×**, validated settings clamp it to **1.25–5×**, and authoritative game settings supply the live multiplier. Bonus statistics track actual extra growth after the existing soft cap and temporary multiplier.
- **Animal Escape:** completing the existing quest grants one ride during the 60-second friendship. This is an availability window, not 60 seconds of invulnerability. Pigeon approaches, enlarges, carries the character along a visible flight, and lands at an authority-chosen location. Cat grows and follows a bounded grid route around major geometry. Both prevent mouth/quest/power interactions during carriage; normal choking prevents activation. A valid active friendship earns a fresh final assist upon transition, even if its normal ride was already used. Expired, hostile and unearned states never carry over.
- **Hell:** a two-second transition leads into a **36×24 platform of 80-unit cells** (2,880×1,920; 864 tiles; **3.086×** the old area). Characters use a shared **2× radius** configuration after the existing reset to starting mass, for humans and bots alike. The following camera uses the expanded bounds; the minimap retains the full arena. Spawn radius is 560. A telegraphed black hole locks each path and speed category; 16% slow, 60% normal, 20% fast and 4% extreme, with speed factors 0.7/1/1.55/2.7. Its base rises from 230 to 390. Extreme warnings last 2 seconds; others 1.1. Twelve percent of attacks use a quadratic curve shared by rendering, simulation and bots. Once per sweep it may pause 0.5–1.5 seconds, sampled only during travel. It continues destroying occupied tiles while paused without resetting destruction timestamps. Cells retain the warning/destruction state machine. Unsupported participants fall for 0.75 seconds; final falling participants share a tie even if animations end on neighboring ticks.
- **Lava eruptions:** first warning at 30 seconds of Hell, then seeded 27–33-second intervals. One or two remaining safe patches glow/crack for 2 seconds, build pressure and erupt for 1.2 seconds. A hit gives one 150-unit knockback over 0.4 seconds, never direct elimination or mass loss. Falling after displacement uses the existing lava rules. Glowing cracks, orange jets, bright cores and 48 pooled sparks render in the existing loop.
- **Hell Speed:** up to three pickups, attempting every 12 seconds. A flood fill from living players rejects disconnected/destroyed/warning ground; pickup locations also avoid the black hole and eruptions. Destroyed-ground items retire immediately. Pickups use the same authoritative 15-second Speed effect. Normal props, quests, revenge, feeding, other powers, respawns and PvP consumption stay outside the finale.
- **Final helpers:** a pigeon carries the player over gaps for five seconds; cat performs a two-second grounded dash, with a gap crossing bounded to 96 units and 0.4 seconds. Pigeon landing searches within 120 units; cat within 80. If no valid nearby ground exists, falling is allowed. Each entitlement is consumed once in shared state. Bots inspect current terrain and the public black-hole telegraph, favor connected safe areas and use helpers when threatened.
- **Review:** per-participant size history, deaths/lives/respawns, Pluto and animal statistics, Hell survival/cause/outcome, and bounded meaningful phase timelines. The existing ledger receives schema-version-3 JSON details including match settings and starting life count and proper draw outcomes through `20261001000000_eat_it_match_settings.sql`; existing rows remain untouched.

Deploy the updated `eat-it-match` Edge Function (its shared imports changed), frontend and new SQL migration together for online play. No remote deployment is performed by local development/testing. Local practice results remain local, as before.

`npm run test:eat-it` includes progression, underpass, Pluto, animal, Hell, eight-bot stress and PGlite result-trigger checks in addition to all existing tests. Development-only browser fixtures are `/tests/eat-it-expansion-preview.html` for renderer scenarios and `/tests/eat-it-ui-preview.html` for respawn/Hell/review UI. These are not production routes. Large models use approximate planar colliders; cat route search can decline an Escape when no clear route exists. Final helper landings perform a short collision correction only; they do not rescue across the arena.

## Stored Jump / Strike and companion update

`abilities.ts` extends the shared player state with separate one-use Jump and Strike charges and one mutually exclusive active motion. **J** uses Jump; **Space** uses Strike. The HUD has separate ready buttons, disabled while conflicting actions are active. 2x Growth and animal Escape retain their existing buttons. Repeat keydown does not consume another charge. Commands carry only `jump` / `strike` intent; the server chooses direction from the member's current input/facing and owns motion, pickup, charge consumption, contacts and stats. The snapshot buffer interpolates ability transforms without simulating rewards or landings.

Jump appears only in Hell. It travels 320 units over 0.95 seconds, with a 125-unit sinusoidal rise. Airborne players cannot eat, collect or be eaten; support is checked again at the exact landing point. Invalid landings fall immediately without a rescue teleport. Hell makes a spawn attempt every 12 seconds with a 20% Jump / 10% Strike / 70% Speed roll. Each rare kind has at most one world pickup, with 24-second Jump / 35-second Strike cooldowns and a global three-item cap. Spawn points must be reachable intact floor, away from players, black holes and eruption warnings. Normal-map Strike has a 4% weight per five-second attempt, a 35-second cooldown, one active world item and no initial pickup.

Strike uses the existing grounded movement and collision flow: a 0.06-second windup followed by a decelerating burst, nominally 180 units in 0.45 seconds. Rim motion and short orange trails communicate the burst. It keeps the 130% radius rule, front mouth fit/contact and shield protection. It grants no lava immunity. Both abilities reject dead, respawning, choking, falling, stunned, escaping, transitioning or already-active actors. Death and the existing Hell reset clear temporary inventory.

Hard bots seek valid prey lines with Strike and use it to flee; Medium/Easy permit less accurate timing/range. Hell bots score visible safe landing endpoints and current gaps for Jump; Easy may spend it early. Grounded Hell escape Strikes require a safe sampled path. Difficulty changes the decision interval and number of samples, never move distance or item ownership.

Raised cars/houses remain passable until the actual devour threshold. At sufficient mouth size, their existing 25% entry, gradual lean and full-size fall continue without an intermediate solid collision. Trees instead begin at the trunk, remain upright until canopy contact, then either finish or visibly choke for three seconds. Spitting first reverses out of the mouth before a short local arc and cooldown; failed tree attempts never grant growth. Cosmetic burp puffs and facial motion remain, with no burp word or localization key.

Friendly cats and pigeons last 60 seconds. Catch-up speed follows player speed/distance; friendly cats can pass underneath supported vehicles/buildings. Every four seconds before expiry, an apple visibly travels from the companion to the mouth and follows normal reward handling. At expiry, the helper entitlement, target and feed deadline clear, then the animal leaves. Active friendship still carries the established final Hell assist (pigeon five seconds, cat two seconds). A compact heart timer shows remaining friendship; scaling remains shared with the player-relative model.

Review adds paired collected/used ability counts, successful gap jumps, Strike-assisted devours, companion seconds/feeds and vehicles eaten alongside existing tree/choke/building counts. All new strings cover the seven existing languages. `tests/eat-it-abilities.test.mjs` verifies the authority and conflict rules; `/tests/eat-it-abilities-preview.html` and the `abilities` option in `/tests/eat-it-ui-preview.html` provide repeatable browser checks.
