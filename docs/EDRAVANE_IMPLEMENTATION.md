**Edravane: playable campaign expansion and verification report**

Implemented in the existing Medieval Kingdoms React/Vite project. The expansion connects structured geography, house ownership, dynasty decisions, resources, trade, army commands, explicit campaign turns, diplomatic and military responses, simultaneous battle rounds, campaign aftermath, challenges, bots, and an authenticated Supabase lobby. This is a first playable implementation; the concrete limits below distinguish its simplified systems from a finished grand-strategy game.

The new entry point is `/games/medieval-kingdoms`. The original world remains at `/games/medieval-kingdoms/legacy`, with all original campaign and battle URLs still available. Original maps, characters, battlefield code, minigames, and the `medieval-kingdoms-campaign-progress-v1` and Moonville save keys are preserved. Changes to legacy screens only redirect their return navigation to the legacy world. A legacy region appears on the new map, using the old map asset; clicking it opens a Legacy Region information card in the sidebar, with “Legacy Region — unavailable in this campaign” and a Visit link to the original world. All campaign movement, ship transport, trade, conquest, and lobby selections reject it.

The supplied concept image is retained as `public/MedievalKingdoms/edravane/concept-map.png` as a reference asset. The playable map now draws its terrain directly inside the actual SVG hex polygons; it no longer places live hexes over the illustration. Forests, mountains, fields, rivers, cities and castles are vector scenery sharing the playable tile coordinates. Rivers connect across their shared tile edges, and coastlines/political boundaries follow the land hex outlines. Geography follows the reference broadly: High Cairn northwest, Varnesk north, Sylvarenne northeast, Dunwald east, Auremarch central, Ilyr Coast southwest, Graskor south, and Saltmere southeast. It does not trace every illustrated coastline exactly. Names are provisional fictional names, without a claim of legal clearance.

The current preset has 180 playable land districts, including six islands, 120 sea fields, and one locked legacy marker. World and regional views use the same odd-row hex coordinates. Map zoom works independently of region selection: +/− buttons and the mouse wheel zoom from 75% to 400%, dragging pans, and Fit map restores the world camera. Selecting a hex changes the sidebar and highlights its actual polygon without forcing a region zoom. House and holding navigation center the relevant seat or estate; return navigation resets the camera. The campaign map fills the viewport, with translucent header, turn, army and sidebar panels. The mobile sidebar can collapse or expand, and inspection remains available outside the player’s turn. A navigation trail, minimap, terrain/ownership/loyalty/resources/trade overlays, reduced-motion control, and keyboard-selectable districts are provided. Thin field borders, medium estate borders, thick political nation borders, gold royal inner outlines and crowns, coloured house crests, occupied hatching, and disputed dashes make control visible. The selected district identifies its owner, liege, resource, infrastructure, unrest, loyalty, obligations, and pledged troops.

Each nation has a crown domain and four distinct minor houses: resource, frontier, trade, and claimant. Forty houses own separate holdings, treasuries, stocks, families, relationships, ambitions, opinions, legitimacy recognition, and military contracts. Starting rulers and two adult children have parent links, claims, eligibility, marriage links, ages, and prisoner status. Marriage creates reciprocal relationships; heirs can succeed by player decision or ruler mortality as campaign years advance. Elven council and Ork assembly recognition require sufficient legitimacy. Supporting a claimant creates hostile rebel forces; a successful rebel battle at its national capital transfers the crown title and lieges. House identity, original house role, district ownership, land provenance, and current title holder remain separate.

Every house has a capital seat with a major city beside a level 2 castle within the same hex. The eight major crown houses begin with 5,000 capital troops and a second seat holding 2,000 troops. Minor houses begin with 2,000 capital troops. These totals include the castle's 500 guards. Secondary seats have a market city and level 1 castle. Additional rural castles begin with 500 troops and are placed beside market cities. Castle guards are part of the stationary reserve pool and are not counted twice.

New campaigns begin with no independent mobile hosts. The map shows buildings, not forty individual marching armies, and the army selector lists available friendly mobile forces. “Raise 500 troops” transfers reserves into a mobile host for 50 coins and 20 food, leaving at least 500 soldiers behind. A house can maintain up to three independent field hosts; summoned service contingents remain separate. Recruitment adds 100 troops for 100–500 coins (depending on unit), 50 food, and the relevant iron/horses. Minor-house reserves can therefore grow above 2,000. Static reserves also eat and receive pay, but do not suffer marching terrain attrition: per 1,000 troops per campaign tick, garrisons consume 0.4 food and 0.2 coins; field hosts consume 2 food and 1 coin.

The header displays coins, food, house troop totals, round and active crown on small and large screens. Own unoccupied farmland offers a manual harvest of 32 food in plains/river fields, otherwise 18, with six completed crown turns of regrowth. Existing grain production and imports also supply food; bots harvest their farms on the same cooldown. A major city produces four coins per campaign tick, a market city two; each city endpoint adds 12 units to a trade route's cargo capacity. The district panel exposes all seat, city, castle, harvest, mobilization, recruitment and upgrade actions.

Campaigns use a fixed eight-crown turn order, starting with the chosen founding crown. A round contains eight crown turns. During their turn, a player may harvest, recruit, trade, propose diplomacy, plan movement and annex eligible occupied territory. End turn commits one hex of movement for that crown's commanded forces and advances the shared economy, routes, upkeep and cooldowns by one campaign tick. Other crowns' marching armies wait for their own turn. Human turns have no wall-clock deadline and do not advance automatically. Bots take their own turns through the same command validator, stopping whenever a human must act or a tactical battle is running. Campaign action controls and server authority enforce turn ownership; map navigation and inspection remain available while waiting.

War declarations interrupt play so the receiving crown can raise, reinforce, summon, upgrade castles and plan defensive movement before choosing Defenses ready or requesting peace. Peace offers require the other crown to accept or decline. Accepting peace cancels the related invasion responses and military paths. Marriage proposals cost a non-refundable 30 coins and name eligible heirs, but create spouse links and relationship bonuses only after acceptance. Rejected offers leave the marriage unset. Responses themselves consume no campaign tick.

An invasion stops before hostile entry or occupation. The receiving crown can choose an eligible defending force on the threatened field or an adjacent mobile force, defend, withdraw, or counteroffer peace. Withdrawal selects uncontested neighboring fields, preferring the defender’s realm, and costs stationed defenders about 2% of their troops (rounded per unit). Defense starts the existing tactical battle while campaign turns wait. Remaining hostile forces receive further responses before occupation can be awarded. Bots evaluate diplomatic relationships and defensive strength, including castle bonuses, to make the same decisions.

The Houses sidebar lets players navigate all forty houses and centers each capital. Profiles show the current ruler, living heirs and next eligible successor, actual troops under each person’s command, direct armies and reserves, sworn vassals with current troop totals and loyalty, and directly controlled castles/cities. Heirs without a command correctly show zero personal troops; house reserves remain separate. Clicking a vassal or holding opens its profile or hex details. Hex details show the current controller, total troops, individual forces, morale, army loyalty, infrastructure and attack options. Declare war & attack is an atomic command: it validates the field army, target, path and reason before changing anything. The receiver’s sidebar automatically opens the threatened hex and its response controls. Own fields occupied by a foreign blocker or rebels are also valid military targets.

A field army with at least one surviving troop can explicitly block foreign trade on its hex, including in peacetime. Hostile garrisons also obstruct passing cargo, and coastal forces can block adjacent maritime lanes. Empty forces and friendly armies do not block their own nation’s routes; mutineers can obstruct their former crown’s trade. Blockade stops cargo progress and delivery; marching, embarking, withdrawing or lifting the order ends an explicit blockade. Route status names the blocking force. Both the player’s routes and sworn vassal routes can justify a crown’s war. Available reasons are derived from current evidence: Trade route blocked, Recover occupied territory, Rescue captured family, or Defend a marriage ally. The last reason requires an active war and a recorded declaration against the ally. Fabricated reasons are rejected by the authority.

An evidenced declaration causes no declaration unrest or loyalty penalty. An unjustified declaration adds five unrest across the crown/vassal realm, lowers royal army loyalty by six, and increments that ruler house’s unjustified-war count. The second and every later unjustified declaration additionally lower vassal opinion by fifteen, reducing their calculated loyalty. The form previews these effects before the command. War history, reasons, counts, army loyalty and blockade orders persist; older saves use an 85% army-loyalty baseline without minting troops or changing existing balances.

Army loyalty is distinct from vassal political loyalty. A vassal’s effective army loyalty is capped by its house loyalty; royal troops use their own army loyalty. Loyalty improves campaign morale recovery and tactical/AI starting combat strength. Hunger and unpaid wages reduce loyalty. A mobile host below 30% loyalty and 40 morale has a seeded mutiny chance on its own crown’s completed turn, up to 20% at zero loyalty. Loyal armies have no mutiny chance, and garrisons do not use this army-mutiny check. Low-loyalty vassals can also rebel through the house uprising rules. Rebel hosts cannot receive royal player commands and can fight their own crown; bots handle their defensive responses, even when the original house belongs to a human. These are starting balance values.

Level 1/2/3 castles give 15%/30%/55% defensive strength bonuses in both tactical combat and AI resolution. Upgrading level 1 to 2 costs 150 coins, upgrading 2 to 3 costs 300; both consume 30 timber and 20 iron. Castle upgrades persist, cannot exceed level 3, and require ownership and an unoccupied estate. Castles remain simplified defended battlefield positions rather than destructible siege walls.

Loyalty is 55% personal opinion plus 45% recognition of legitimacy; military obligations are a separate troop count. Claimant warnings, refusal reasons, concessions, partial contribution, delays, and service expiry are visible. A summons detaches a contracted contingent rather than granting command of a vassal's full army. Low loyalty refuses service; middling loyalty supplies half the obligation after three ticks. Pledges last 24 campaign ticks by default, after which surviving troops return to house control. Independent friendly commanders evaluate requested objectives. Royal armies and active pledged contingents accept direct commands; other armies and opposing forces do not. Authority uses a house ID rather than assuming that every actor holds a crown, preparing the core for later minor-house players.

Economy produces and consumes grain, timber, iron, livestock, horses, herbs, and luxury goods. Grain sustains armies and households; livestock feeds households; iron and horses equip recruitment; timber builds shipyards and ships; herbs aid resting armies; income pays troops. Supply shortages and unpaid wages reduce combat effectiveness. Resource stock is capped at 999; prices use explainable world-stock calculations bounded to 1–12 coins. The ledger shows stock, price, and shortages. Estates pay taxes and armies incur upkeep.

Starting balance constants in `world.ts` apply once to qualifying land/infrastructure: Auremarch grain +20%, High Cairn mountain iron +20%, Dunwald forest timber +20%, Sylvarenne woodland herbs +15%, Graskor grazing livestock +20%, Saltmere established shipyard ship costs −15%, and qualifying foreign Ilyr luxury exports +15% income. Varnesk army origin grants −20% winter attrition. Annexation preserves the original district's economic provenance, and internal transfers do not create the Ilyr export bonus. Glaciers, volcanic fields, desert, and marshes have explicit movement and attrition hazards.

Players approve automated import or export contracts. Land caravans start at 12 units, maritime merchants 24; city endpoint bonuses add to these capacities. Routes require an owned origin, a legal destination, affordable construction, and a connected land or sea path. Maritime routes need two ports and an origin shipyard. Dotted cyan live sea routes show moving merchant indicators; delivery accounts for transport costs, available cargo, buyer funds, storm loss, hostile interception, and blockades. Army transports make island campaigns playable: each ship carries up to 80 troops and costs timber and coins, with coastal interception and configured maritime hazards. Use imports early when ruling a food-poor coastal or mountainous crown.

Hostile movement creates queued responses and encounters in stable army-ID order. Armies already in an encounter stop moving. Defenders can reinforce, fight or withdraw before invasion; campaign time remains frozen throughout an encounter and its battle. New battles use five troop types—footsoldiers, spearmen, archers, heavy infantry and cavalry—with front, rear and flank deployment. Each side commits one order per round: Hold, Advance, Flank, Volley or Retreat. Both plans resolve simultaneously; opposing plans remain private in multiplayer until resolution. Elapsed wall time cannot resolve a round. Spearmen counter cavalry, rear archers benefit from protection, flanking cavalry can reach exposed archers, and difficult terrain and castles stop cavalry flanks. Unit counters, terrain, fatigue, supply, loyalty-derived morale and castle defenses affect actual losses. The UI explains counters and round outcomes. AI uses the same round engine, including automatic AI-only resolution and disconnected-player takeover. Existing saved battles without round metadata retain their original tactical behavior until completion.

Casualties separate healthy troops, wounded and deaths. Wounded cannot fight, consume supplies and remain with surviving armies; they recover on supplied friendly land on their own crown’s turn (cities 25%, castles 20%, camps 10%). Morale reacts to casualties, rear attacks, food shortages, supporting units, victories and commander incapacitation; badly depleted or low-morale formations rout. A friendly city supports the defender with food and permits one 100-soldier reinforcement from an eligible nearby garrison for 10 coins and 10 food, retaining at least 500 healthy garrison troops. Battles end on rout, retreat or a 20-round limit. The preview estimates relative strength rather than a guaranteed win chance.

Results update troop counts, wounds, morale, retreats, occupation, commander prisoners, legitimacy and opinions. The outcome panel reports healthy survivors, wounded, dead and captured troops, with round explanations. Result IDs prevent duplicate aftermath application. Occupation can be annexed after three campaign ticks with a controlled garrison and a 50-coin cost. Battles charge no additional campaign tick; the frozen interval produces no resources, merchant progress, movement or challenge cooldown progress. Campaign planning waits for End turn.

Every starting crown has reachable gold and silver fields. Gold offers a generated merchant-bargaining arithmetic puzzle for 60 coins. Silver offers a tactical decision tournament for 20 levies. One attempt is permitted per challenge; incorrect answers also trigger a 30-tick cooldown. Successful rewards are limited to three per house/field for the campaign lifetime. Challenge answers, ownership, cooldowns, reward caps, and one-time completion are checked in the authoritative simulation and persist in saves.

Multiplayer now uses the existing Supabase project rather than a separately hosted Node/WebSocket server. The authenticated `edravane-match` Edge Function executes the shared game rules and stores campaigns in Postgres. Clients send commands and fetch personalized snapshots; Realtime distributes only room revision notices. Full campaign rows and private battle plans are inaccessible through browser database queries. Atomic version checks prevent concurrent requests from overwriting a turn. Eight crown slots support humans and bots, readiness including the host, sea-hazard settings, and host migration. Every accepted change is persisted immediately. Human turns and committed battle rounds never advance from elapsed wall time.

Players use their existing Supabase accounts. A database heartbeat renews only the caller's seat every 30 seconds. After 90 seconds without a heartbeat, an active player's next synchronization marks the missing player disconnected and gives that crown to bots. A disconnected host transfers to another connected human. If everyone is offline, the saved campaign waits until someone returns. Reconnect or joining the same code with the same account restores its original seat; the code is remembered in the current browser tab. No dedicated Edravane server process, socket URL or reconnect token is required. Old Node-server rooms are not automatically imported. See [EDRAVANE_MULTIPLAYER.md](EDRAVANE_MULTIPLAYER.md) for deployment and operating details.

Single-player saves use `medieval-kingdoms-edravane-v2` with `version: 2` and `world: Edravane`. Save, Continue, JSON export/import, and saving on return to the campaign menu are implemented. Earlier Edravane v2 geography snapshots are accepted without rewriting their playable geography. Saves carry `estateRules: 1`; older local and persisted multiplayer campaigns gain seat/building metadata and empty reserve slots while retaining existing mobile forces and economic balances. Starting 5,000/2,000 troop allocations apply to newly created campaigns, so migration does not mint troops or erase an older army. Turn order, current round, in-progress End turn and pending reactions persist in saves. Older local and server campaigns without turn metadata gain it while retaining economic balances, troops and the existing tick. Invalid turn and reaction metadata is rejected. Multiplayer state is owned by the Edge Function and cannot be replaced by a client save. Postgres commits the room, membership status and revision notice atomically before replying. Reloads and Edge Function cold starts retain accepted game progress.

Run the frontend with `npm run dev` and open `/games/medieval-kingdoms`. Multiplayer requires the normal `VITE_SUPABASE_URL` and `VITE_SUPABASE_PUBLISHABLE_KEY`, the Edravane database migration and the deployed `edravane-match` function. Sign in, select a crown, open Multiplayer council, host or join by code, ready every human including the host, and start. Unoccupied seats are bots. The Edravane migration and Edge Function are deployed to the linked existing project. This change does not publish the frontend to a public website; publish it through the application's existing hosting workflow. Single-player remains local.

Remaining limitations are concrete:

- The playable geography is a stylized vector hex map rather than the original painted illustration. Cities and castle footprints sit beside each other within a seat hex; they are not separately selectable building parcels.
- New battles use aggregate troop blocks and three deployment zones rather than individual soldier movement. Castle defense is a strength modifier rather than a destructible siege system. The same deterministic engine resolves human and AI battles; the displayed percentage is relative estimated strength, not a calibrated victory probability. Existing ongoing legacy battles keep their original movement and obstacle simulation.
- Dynasty families begin with a ruler and two adult children. Marriage, inheritance, mortality, claims, and captivity work, but births, extended genealogy generation, prisoner ransom/release, territorial peace terms, and complex council voting are not implemented. Council/assembly recognition is a legitimacy threshold.
- Minor-house authority is supported by the simulation, but the public lobby and campaign selector expose eight founding crown houses only. There is no minor-house selection or privilege-negotiation screen yet.
- Sea warfare uses transport risk and coastal interception/blockade. There are no separately commanded fleets or tactical naval battles. Bots use simple strategic rules; they do not optimize trade, coordinate alliances, or build an advanced navy.
- Gold and silver minigames are bounded decision puzzles, not animated bargaining scenes or a second full tactical tournament engine.
- Supabase multiplayer uses account authentication, saved rooms and Realtime revision notices. Spectator-only seats and large-scale WAN/load testing are not supplied. The existing Supabase plan limits apply; removing the dedicated Edravane server does not guarantee unlimited free usage. Each account can create six councils per hour. An offline campaign resumes when a seated account returns; bot takeover is detected by an active client rather than an always-running worker.
- Resource, attrition, AI aggressiveness, supply, and combat constants are starting values and have not undergone extended balance/playtesting. A player can lose their army or exhaust their dynasty; a formal campaign victory/defeat screen and restart flow beyond the campaign menu are not implemented.
