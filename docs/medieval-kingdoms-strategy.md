# Medieval Kingdoms: Edravane strategy expansion

The existing map campaign now connects dynastic politics, logistics, war settlements and kingdom identity. These changes apply to single-player and the shared multiplayer simulation.

## Player-facing systems

- **Characters and advisers:** deterministic personalities and four skills. Command affects combat; diplomacy improves vassal bargains; stewardship affects income and provision capacity; intrigue extends scout visibility at 16 skill. Marshal, chancellor, steward and spymaster appointments contribute their rulers' skills. Proud rulers resent insults, greedy rulers value economic concessions, loyal rulers support succession, and ambitious/cautious/brave bot rulers make different decisions.
- **Feudal bargains:** vassals request lower taxes, an office, a frontier estate or protected trade. Tax and land concessions increase troop obligations. Land grants transfer actual garrison reserves. Trade promises are remembered and penalize opinion when a blockade breaks them. Ignored demands gradually reduce opinion.
- **Dynasties:** primogeniture, partition, elective and clan laws; nominations; council support previews; regencies for minors; cadet houses under partition; marriages with explicit heirs and crown consent; births and inherited claims. Adult inherited foreign claims can justify war once the living marriage pact no longer protects that realm.
- **Supply and seasons:** friendly grain farms, cities and depots supply hosts through accessible districts. Hostile forces close corridors. Access treaties open foreign supply paths; shipyards can resupply friendly ports across open sea lanes. Field hosts carry three food turns (four for Varnesk), consumed only on their controlling realm's turn. The route preview warns about shortages before arrival. Winter increases food use and harsh-terrain attrition.
- **Intelligence:** own estates, hosts, watchtowers, scouts and successful trade provide visibility. Unseen armies are omitted from multiplayer snapshots. Previous contacts remain approximate and stale for up to five rounds; opponents' future marching orders and private scout reports are omitted. Scouts cost 25 coins, reach six hexes from the realm and remain through two rounds.
- **Sieges:** an adjacent host of at least 100 soldiers can blockade an enemy castle at war. Food runs down; engines require three siege turns, 20 timber and 10 iron. Assaults cost 12% of attackers before combat, or 3% with engines. Surrender requires the defender's response. Lift the siege before marching or sailing. Relief battles can break a siege.
- **Peace:** recorded war objectives and leverage from occupied estates and prisoners. Terms include white peace, restored occupation, prisoner release, protected trade, estate cession, a claimant crown or tribute. Reparations and mutual military access can be included. Claimant installation requires 60 leverage; tribute requires 20. Accepted treaties enforce a six-round truce; tribute pays 15 coins on exactly three debtor turns. Land and money transfer only after acceptance.
- **Kingdom identities:** grain-rich Auremarch; stronger hill/mountain castles in High Cairn; higher maritime revenue in Saltmere; longer watchtower range in Dunwald; extra provisions and reduced winter attrition in Varnesk; council recognition in Sylvarenne; stronger marriage relations on the Ilyr Coast; battle prestige and assembly legitimacy in Graskor.

## Interface

World zoom shows curved realm names that grow with currently controlled territory. Original SVG shields distinguish all great and minor houses on fields they control; regional zoom adds house names and shields on separated holdings. Labels follow occupation and independent rebellions. Saltmere’s crown label stays centered on its island capital. Regional zoom also reveals house boundaries, terrain and observed host counts. Estate zoom adds local labels and details. Supply, diplomacy and inherited-claim map modes join the existing modes. Context panels provide actionable siege, building, succession, marriage and peace decisions, with prerequisite explanations. The chronicle records important political and military events and links back to their houses and places. Mobile controls scroll horizontally, and the expandable sidebar contains the same decision panels and supply preview.

Wheel and button zoom stop at 100%, the responsive fit of the playable kingdoms and islands between the floating panels. Reaching this limit recenters the camera after panning. The locked legacy field is excluded from the campaign fit; it remains on the world minimap and can still be reached by panning.

The map fills the viewport and fits land between floating controls. The translucent realm desk can move between the left and right sea edges, expand, or close. Original geometric SVG badges show signed effects for war declarations, insults, vassal bargains and succession laws; labels accompany the icons and colors. Solid gold boundaries identify the crown's direct domain; warm dotted boundaries identify vassal estates, using current occupation control. Own hosts use solid markers, independent vassal hosts use dotted markers, and temporarily pledged contingents use paired rings.

Selecting a vassal estate offers a host request. At 55 loyalty or above, a vassal uses an available independent host or raises up to 1,000 soldiers, bounded by its obligation and reserves. Raising uses the vassal's own coins and grain and retains 500 castle guards. March and hold requests leave direct authority with the vassal; refusal preserves its previous orders and resources. Existing summons still provide bounded, temporary pledge control, clearly labeled in the interface.

## Expanded geography

New campaigns have 814 hexes, including 333 land districts, across two separate continents and Saltmere’s six original island fields. The crowns retain their original capitals and relative positions. The western continent contains Auremarch, High Cairn, Varnesk, the Ilyr Coast and Graskor; the eastern continent contains Sylvarenne and Dunwald, with Saltmere controlling southern coastal ports as well as its archipelago. Every Saltmere field has a port. Open sea separates the continents: ships and maritime trade can cross, while land armies need transport.

Clicking a field centers a regional view at 180% zoom; dragging, arrow controls, wheel zoom and Fit map remain available. Local settlement text uses the bundled IM Fell English Regular font (SIL OFL 1.1; license and source record accompany the asset). Camera fitting and minimaps use the actual saved geography, including negative coordinates. Memoized field and terrain layers, bucketed viewport culling with overscan, a land-only minimap, and one camera update per animation frame reduce drawing work during navigation. Close-up settlement names share one canvas layer instead of hundreds of clipped SVG text containers. A bounded cache rasterizes each distinct name once per resolution and reuses those images while panning; names still wrap within their fields and use the bundled medieval font. The layer does not intercept hex clicks. Wheel events are combined into one update per animation frame, with transform easing disabled during scrolling. Expensive map filters and panel blur are suspended while dragging or scrolling. Indexed adjacency keeps pathfinding practical on the larger map. Existing 241- and 301-hex saves retain their original geography; start a new campaign to use the expanded world.

## Compatibility and operation

Single-player v2 saves migrate by adding `strategyRules: 1` and optional system metadata; existing progress, troops and balances are preserved. New strategy fields are validated on import. Multiplayer filtering is shared by the Supabase council handler and legacy WebSocket server.

Run `npm run check:edravane` and `npm run test:edravane`. The regression suite covers turn authority, atomic rejection, consent, conservation of troops/stocks, supply, hidden information, succession, siege aftermath, treaty effects, save migration and an 18-round bot campaign.

The changes are local source changes. To update hosted multiplayer, deploy the existing `edravane-match` Supabase function with its shared simulation imports, or restart/redeploy the legacy Edravane server. Publish the frontend through the project's normal deployment workflow. Balance values are an initial playable tuning and should be adjusted after human playtesting.
