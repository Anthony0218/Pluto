# Eat It

Open `/games/eat-it`, choose City, Nature, Candy or Frozen, choose 1–8 participants (turn Bots OFF for solo exploration), then start a bot game or an authenticated multiplayer room. WASD/arrow keys move; dragging creates a floating joystick for touch and mouse. Escape pauses solo play. With Lives enabled, a participant with lives remaining can respawn after five seconds; permanently eliminated participants spectate, with an immediate Restart action in solo games. F3 shows colliders, direction, velocity, bot state and the radius threshold in development builds only.

## Architecture and reused systems

- The existing React Router tree lazy-loads the game. `src/data/games.ts` supplies the games page, landing cards, navigation, favorites and planets.
- React renders menus, rooms, results and the HUD (10 Hz). Three.js WebGL renders both arenas through `requestAnimationFrame`, using a tilted orthographic camera, directional lighting, shadows and procedural 3D models. Ground textures are drawn locally once per map; labels and the minimap use an overlay canvas. The existing Three.js dependency is reused; no physics package or downloaded asset was added.
- Serializable TypeScript rules run at a fixed 30 Hz in solo play and the Supabase Edge Function. A square-root mass-to-radius function and one Hell scale configuration control logical size. Camera zoom and transforms are smoothed; the rendered mouth radius matches authoritative size exactly.
- Food has planar velocity, friction, pushes and an independent height/vertical velocity for falling and bouncing. Player-to-prop collisions use rotated rectangular footprints, with diagonal circles only for broad-phase checks. Food consumption compares footprint area with mouth area, then checks that the object center is over the visible opening, at lip height or falling, with line-of-sight. Fitting props receive no player-body pushes, so approaching or turning over a snack does not kick it away. Player bites retain their front-entry and inward-motion rules. A claimed object records the direction of lost ground support, tips around the remaining outer lip and accelerates into depth. Models retain their full size. Circular holes discard the ground surface, allowing depth-tested occlusion of the portions below the rim; one reward is granted after the fall. Ordinary food has no remote attraction. Victims are eliminated immediately by the authority and animated locally for 0.56 seconds.
- Spawn management starts with 720 props (hard cap 1,000) and caps ground power-ups at 9. It cycles map sectors, rejects solid/player/food overlaps and respects the closing boundary.
- Bots use only nearby visible players, food and power-ups. They forage, flee with a sideways escape, hunt, seek power-ups and reposition around obstacles/the closing ring. They receive no mass or movement advantages.
- Supabase auth/profile names, the established Edge Function + optimistic room version + Postgres Realtime pattern, existing friend messages/dialogs, shared language store and shared audio settings are reused. No client-host authority or extra socket service exists.
- Input requests are bounded to one in flight per client, normally every 125 ms, with a 5-second request timeout and cancellation on exit. Clients submit direction, Growth activation, Escape or Respawn intent only. Server-clock fixed steps, normalized inputs, membership checks, shield checks, and compare-and-swap retries protect state. A bounded snapshot buffer interpolates remote transforms 120 ms behind receipt, with at most 160 ms of movement extrapolation. Local movement is predicted for responsiveness; mass, bites, rewards and eliminations are never predicted. Stale input stops after 650 ms and a disconnected human forfeits after 20 seconds. Departed/disconnected hosts transfer ownership. Result recording retains all original humans; the next lobby removes departed seats.
- Lobby reads are idempotent apart from infrequent heartbeats, so receiving a Realtime update does not create an endless write/read cycle. Concurrent joins retry enough times for all eight seats. Navigation or account changes remount the room session instead of carrying an old match into a new route.
- Finished online matches write once into the existing immutable `user_game_results` ledger through a database trigger. The profile's existing aggregate shows Eat It games and wins. Detailed result fields are stored in its `details` JSON. Local bot games are explicitly practice and do not submit unverified profile stats.
- Audio is procedural Web Audio (snacks, bites/chews/gulps, collisions, power-ups, elimination, victory and light City/Nature ambience), using the site's master/effects/music/mute settings. Audio starts after a user gesture.
- All UI strings use the existing translation lookup. Core game labels/results have all seven site languages; additional instructions have English/German, with the existing language fallback for missing entries.

## Balance

All values live in `config.ts`. Starting mass 36 gives radius 24. Radius is `max(14, 4 * sqrt(mass))`, with no upper radius clamp; Hell multiplies the shared body/mouth radius by 2 after the existing survival-size reset. Eating another player requires **1.15× radius**, equivalent to **1.3225× mass** before caps/effects, plus front mouth contact, inward motion, physical fit and no shield. Player mass transfer remains 70%.

The mouth diameter is 82% of body diameter. Food fits when its footprint area (`width * height`) is at most the circular mouth area (`π * (0.82 * radius)²`). Area sets eligibility; oversized tree canopies and too-tall leaning props can jam, causing a brief 1.5-second choke and a spit-out. Monster factories are exempt from tall-prop wedging. Loose props start when their center enters the opening. Fitting large props commit at 25% normalized radial entry, measured across their rotated footprint; outward-moving props cannot commit. Shadows follow the rotated object silhouette and do not contribute to its area. Weight controls inertia; growth is separate metadata. The catalog contains 40+ distinct props across six tiers, including map-specific vehicles and buildings. Ordinary rewards soften above 1800 mass; humans and monster factories retain their base rewards. Buildings, vehicles and heavy props tip/fall for 1.8 seconds after valid entry. Small props swallow over 0.95 seconds. Trees descend upright: 0.65 seconds of trunk entry, then canopy contact; a fully fitting tree completes in 2.1 seconds.

Speed lasts 15 seconds (1.5×), Shield 15 seconds and Magnet 15 seconds. Duplicate timed pickups refresh duration without stacking strength. The rare 2x Growth collectible stores one manual activation for a 20-second reward multiplier. `/2` immediately halves future growth for the current life; repeated dividers do not compound. Modifiers multiply, so `/2` with active 2x returns to ordinary rewards. Expiration changes only future rewards, never earned mass. Temporary Size and the generic +30 Growth Boost are removed, including their runtime state and localization. Only Magnet attracts unclaimed eligible props, within its 180-unit extra reach; buildings remain excluded.

The four maps use different weighted object distributions; Nature adds ground drag. Small props bounce more, large props have heavier inertia, and buildings have no bounce. Sleeping props skip integration. A spatial grid handles awake prop collisions, while the eight-player mouth checks use cheap bounds first. The simulation caps objects at 1,000, power-ups at 9 and particles at 96. The renderer runs independently from React; the HUD updates at 10 Hz.

Match duration is 2, 10 (default), or 20 minutes. At timeout, remaining contenders enter Hell if enabled, otherwise the established mass-then-score result rules apply, including genuine ties. The old closing-ring behavior remains only for legacy snapshots without a match-duration setting; it cannot prematurely collapse a configured ten/twenty-minute match. A last-survivor victory can finish either mode earlier. The live HUD separately ranks rounded Growth, then remaining lives, with stable seat order for display only.

## Operational limits

The existing backend uses request-driven Edge Functions, not a continuously running simulation process. Rooms advance as connected clients send heartbeats; after an outage, catch-up is bounded to one second and disconnected players forfeit. Every accepted input currently persists a full snapshot. The 8 Hz request rate and Realtime/database bandwidth need hosted two-browser and eight-human load testing before a public launch. This implementation does not deploy remote schema/functions automatically. There is no ranked matchmaking, group-room sharing, or solo cloud history. Planar collisions use simplified footprints; the 3D fall uses a deterministic supported-edge approximation with gravity-like acceleration, rather than a general-purpose rigid-body/contact solver. Area-based eligibility remains unchanged. Some elongated objects can therefore be swallowed even if their flat footprint would not fit a real circular opening. WebGL2 is required. Renderer disposal releases GPU resources on exit. Water remains impassable terrain. The City shrine is protected until its quest is delivered or removed, then enters the physical food catalog; ordinary raised props belong to the finite-mass food catalog.

## Expanded maps and NPC encounters

Both maps now measure **4000 × 3040** (previously 3200 × 2400), approximately 1.58 times the previous playable area. Spawn rejection, the closing boundary, NPC movement, bots, camera clamps, ground plane and minimap use the new bounds. Twelve sectors interleave four weighted districts: City market/park/residential/street and Nature meadow/forest/cabin/rocky areas. Large props receive additional player-spawn clearance. New `smallTree` and `treeTrunk` props bridge progression. Props retain fixed world dimensions after spawning.

Every ordinary prop has finite inverse mass, including buildings. Oversized player contact applies a high effective resistance, capped at a 0.00002 object share of the correction/impulse. The player takes virtually all collision response. Heavy props can still receive impulses from other physical props, integrate motion and fall into a sufficiently large mouth. Sleeping-body optimization and the prop spatial grid remain. Bots test rotated footprints instead of enclosing circles so long props do not falsely close corridors.

`treeGeometry` defines the model's trunk radius (6.5% of width) and horizontal canopy semiaxes (46% of width, 40% of depth). The mouth-fit helper compares these radii with the same circular opening rendered on screen; vertical height and rectangular empty corners do not count as foliage. Trunk fit starts an upright vertical descent. After 0.65 seconds, the canopy reaches the mouth and its width is checked. If it fails, the player is stunned for **3 seconds**, loses voluntary movement and new mouth/quest interactions, but remains vulnerable. The leaves remain stuck above the mouth for those three seconds. A deterministic nearby placement search then sends the intact tree through a 0.85-second reverse-out and tumbling arc; no tree reward is granted. A short reacquisition cooldown prevents immediate repeated capture. Bare tree trunks use ordinary area-based eating. The renderer shows compression, shaking, enlarged distressed eyes and cough marks; the HUD shows a localized countdown.

`quests.ts` owns one serializable encounter per map. The NPC phase is a single state: idle, wandering, flying (pigeon only), running (cat only), swallowing, devoured, emerging, friendly, hostile, leaving or gone. Timers use simulation time. Neutral movement uses seeded destinations, smooth turning and ground obstacle checks. Flying pigeons cannot enter mouths; grounded NPCs require visible mouth overlap and sufficient area capacity. Quest proximity is derived, with 30-unit UI hysteresis, rather than a conflicting NPC phase.

A random City shrine reserves a clear area before props spawn. The **Golden Scroll / Goldene Pergamentrolle** sits beside it. Nature receives a **Cat Tree / Katzenkratzbaum**. Quest items live outside the food/physics arrays and cannot be eaten or magnetized. A thin three-segment arm appears nearby; only the shared hand endpoint touching the item can claim it. Body overlap alone does not. The item then follows the hand outside the body. Only one owner ID exists. Handover requires hand proximity to a neutral NPC and immediately records one completion.

Friendly NPCs follow the recipient for **60 seconds**. Every **4 seconds**, they carry/toss an apple over 0.65 seconds from their actual position toward the mouth, then send it with **3× normal growth** into the existing airborne → mouth-entry → falling → consumption flow (fourteen possible feeds before expiry, hard food cap respected). These apples are reserved to the recipient; missed food remains available to that player. The bonus is attached only to food earned during the friendly interval. The visible apple arc connects the NPC to the reward. No size is added per animation frame.

Eating a neutral NPC uses a 0.48-second swallow and grants a small one-time snack reward (8 pigeon / 12 cat base mass), then hides the NPC for **2 seconds**. It re-emerges for 0.65 seconds: pigeon flies upward, cat jumps. The larger red-eyed hostile form pursues for **20 seconds**, attempting a hit at **5, 10, 15 and 20 seconds**. Hits require face proximity and stun for **1 second**, without reducing mass. Pigeons dive, cats briefly jump, and both retreat between attacks. Squashed eyes, a shaking rim, stars and scratch marks communicate the brief injury. Ground obstacles can let players evade the cat. Both NPCs enter leaving, then the terminal gone state with no target or pending action timer.

Eliminating/disconnecting a carrier returns the undelivered item to its reserved original location. NPC target elimination cancels its encounter reward/revenge. Swallowing the neutral NPC intentionally retires its quest item, including any carried copy, because that NPC no longer accepts a handover. Match completion retires items and NPC activity immediately. Already earned food becomes ordinary food if its recipient is eliminated. Reconnecting clients receive the current authoritative state; they do not restart timers or rewards. Older running snapshots without an encounter continue safely; start a new match to obtain an NPC.

`QuestVisuals` adds pooled procedural NPC, item, shrine and arm meshes to the existing Three.js scene. Shared `eyeProportion` decreases gradually from about 19% of body radius for starters to about 11% for large monsters; whites, pupils and highlights remain visible while the mouth keeps its 82% radius. Both human and bot models use it. React still only updates the HUD at 10 Hz. English, German, Spanish and Portuguese cover all added player-facing text.

NPC position/phase, spawns, ownership, completion, reward food, stun and cleanup run in `stepGame` on the same Edge Function authority. Existing room version comparisons protect concurrent updates. Snapshot interpolation smooths only NPC transforms; it cannot apply an attack or a reward. Deploy the updated `eat-it-match` function together with the frontend; no new table or migration is required.

## Readability and rule preferences

Bots, Animals, Hell Sudden Death and Lives default ON. The menu persists validated preferences under `eat-it-settings`; multiplayer reads only server-created room settings. Animals OFF omits the whole encounter and shrine. Lives OFF starts all participants with one life and prevents respawns. Solo bot difficulty defaults to Medium. Hard retains the previous policy (0.18 s decisions, 0.2 s pursuit prediction, 16 Hell directions); Medium uses 0.42 s decisions and reduced pursuit, Easy 0.75 s and a stronger nearby-food preference. Hell warning reaction delays are 0 / 0.3 / 0.65 s respectively. No movement/mass advantage is attached to difficulty.

Animal scale is `1 + 2.2 * clamp((radius - 24) / 288, 0, 1)^0.7`, with multipliers 1 neutral, 1.15 friendly, 1.8 hostile and 2.8 Escape. The shared authority stores the NPC scale using its target/owner, otherwise the nearest living participant in stable player order. Rewards never depend on scale. Ground power-ups scale only for the viewer (cap 2.2×), including bounded colored glow and indicators; collection radius/effect values stay unchanged. Quest items use a 3.4× cap, the carrier's size when held, and viewer size on the ground. Carried arm reach grows, with thickness capped at 2.4 world units.

Each character has a serializable 27–33 s cosmetic burp timer, staggered initially and after respawn without consuming the gameplay RNG. Major animations delay/cancel burps. Hell uses wider, black-hole-tracking eyes and edge trembling instead. High-contrast Canvas plaques and rounded cream HUD text have dark backgrounds/outlines and colored accents; CSS motion respects reduced-motion preferences.

The black hole uses the locked authoritative straight/curved warning path (1.1 seconds, or 2 seconds for extreme attacks). Layered rings and 40 instanced orbiting particles surround the dark center. A pulsing red/cream laser and arrowhead disappear at sweep start. No extra postprocessing shader or React world-object overlays are added.

## Growth rebalance and review

`grantGrowth` applies every reward source through `base × permanent × temporary`. Food keeps its existing soft cap above 1,800 mass; Pluto and animal bonuses compose with the same modifiers. Player-consumption rewards also use this helper. `countCollected` stores aggregate kind counts, not pickup histories. Existing JSON result storage automatically retains the additional counts, total earned growth, activation count and hostile-hit count; old rows need no backfill. Review uses thumbnails rendered from `ModelLibrary`, the same procedural props used in play, and the shared power icons. Historical hostile size loss remains visible only when a legacy result actually records it.

Rare power spawn weights are **2% 2x / 1% divider per five-second attempt**, no initial rare items, maximum one world item per kind, and **60/90-second minimum spawn cooldowns**. When the owner already has a charge, another 2x stays available in the world. One new charge may be stored during an active window, but cannot extend it until that window ends. Death and Hell's existing survival reset clear both stored charges and per-life penalties. Removed saved item types/timers are discarded safely on shared simulation steps.

Easy bots use stored growth early, Medium waits for a couple of nearby meals, and Hard waits for a cluster/high-value meal or friendly feeding. Hard/Medium steer away from visible dividers. Hell bots assess visible path segments, current floor, revealed eruption warnings and reachable Speed pickups. They never inspect future RNG or pending pause outcomes.

The React HUD remains at 10 Hz. Growth popups read authoritative deltas from the bounded event buffer, last 1.4 seconds and show at most six at once. Park blocks and central alleys keep routes open. Seeded building choices, vehicle types, props and power-ups retain variation. New catalog shapes cover cups, phones, cones, boxes/crates, skateboards, hydrants, carts, acorns, pinecones, nests, tents and hay bales. The released shrine uses the same models, fit tests, animation and review counts as ordinary heavy food.

Growth no longer stops at mass 3,600/radius 240. The shared square-root size model has no upper clamp. Mouth geometry, physics and renderer all derive from `playerRadius`; camera zoom only partially offsets early growth and stops zooming out at 0.62. The minimum radius 14 only applies below ordinary starting mass. Extremely artificial masses can exceed the arena itself; the game does not promise a safe respawn when no free space exists.

Large-object entry is `clamp((mouthRadius + projectedHalfExtent - centerDistance) / (2 * projectedHalfExtent), 0, 1)`. At 0.25, valid fit and non-outward relative motion reserve the object authoritatively. The existing world-space fall direction and full-size mesh rotate gradually, move into the mouth and fall below its depth mask. No pre-entry force was added. Magnet remains the sole attraction mechanic.

Shield remains 30 seconds. Its Canvas overlay now draws a translucent bubble, highlight, six orbiting hexagons, impact ripple and final-four-second pulse. The renderer reads an authority-set contact timestamp and never alters protection.

New Hell matches contain three independent deterministic paths with spaced initial locations, staggered first warnings and destination-separation attempts. Each has its own speed, pause and optional curve. Floor/lava meshes and geometry/materials remain shared. Path buffers update only when their locked path changes. All paths appear on the minimap. Finished eruptions pass through the same idempotent tile-collapse state as black-hole damage, and never restore ground. Bots inspect all three paths and current eruption danger. Pigeon flight lasts five seconds; cat retains its two-second grounded assist. Landing remains local and prefers intact, eruption-free ground away from announced paths.

Bots OFF is stored in `eat-it-settings`, hides count/difficulty controls and removes AI seats before simulation creation. A one-player match continues to its selected timer. With Hell enabled, its objective is to survive 60 seconds after the transition; a lava death is a loss, not a one-person tie. Lives, quests, Pluto, effects and growth remain active. Online bot-fill behavior is unchanged.

Replenishment attempts change from 0.22 to 0.15 seconds and can fill the spare capacity even before anything is eaten. Sky drops begin 280–460 units above ground, weighted 60% tiny/small, 32% medium and 8% large. Buildings, vehicles and shrines never sky-drop. ## Stored Jump / Strike and companion update

`abilities.ts` extends the shared player state with separate one-use Jump and Strike charges and one mutually exclusive active motion. **J** uses Jump; **Space** uses Strike. The HUD has separate ready buttons, disabled while conflicting actions are active. 2x Growth and animal Escape retain their existing buttons. Repeat keydown does not consume another charge. Commands carry only `jump` / `strike` intent; the server chooses direction from the member's current input/facing and owns motion, pickup, charge consumption, contacts and stats. The snapshot buffer interpolates ability transforms without simulating rewards or landings.

Jump appears only in Hell. It travels 320 units over 0.95 seconds, with a 125-unit sinusoidal rise. Airborne players cannot eat, collect or be eaten; support is checked again at the exact landing point. Invalid landings fall immediately without a rescue teleport. Hell makes a spawn attempt every 12 seconds with a 20% Jump / 10% Strike / 70% Speed roll. Each rare kind has at most one world pickup, with 24-second Jump / 35-second Strike cooldowns and a global three-item cap. Spawn points must be reachable intact floor, away from players, black holes and eruption warnings. Normal-map Strike has a 4% weight per five-second attempt, a 35-second cooldown, one active world item and no initial pickup.

Strike uses the existing grounded movement and collision flow: a 0.06-second windup followed by a decelerating burst, nominally 180 units in 0.45 seconds. Rim motion and short orange trails communicate the burst. It keeps the 130% radius rule, front mouth fit/contact and shield protection. It grants no lava immunity. Both abilities reject dead, respawning, choking, falling, stunned, escaping, transitioning or already-active actors. Death and the existing Hell reset clear temporary inventory.

Hard bots seek valid prey lines with Strike and use it to flee; Medium/Easy permit less accurate timing/range. Hell bots score visible safe landing endpoints and current gaps for Jump; Easy may spend it early. Grounded Hell escape Strikes require a safe sampled path. Difficulty changes the decision interval and number of samples, never move distance or item ownership.

Raised cars/houses remain passable until the actual devour threshold. At sufficient mouth size, their existing 25% entry, gradual lean and full-size fall continue without an intermediate solid collision. Trees instead begin at the trunk, remain upright until canopy contact, then either finish or visibly choke for three seconds. Spitting first reverses out of the mouth before a short local arc and cooldown; failed tree attempts never grant growth. Cosmetic burp puffs and facial motion remain, with no burp word or localization key.

Friendly cats and pigeons last 60 seconds. Catch-up speed follows player speed/distance; friendly cats can pass underneath supported vehicles/buildings. Every four seconds before expiry, an apple visibly travels from the companion to the mouth and follows normal reward handling. At expiry, the helper entitlement, target and feed deadline clear, then the animal leaves. Active friendship still carries the established final Hell assist (pigeon five seconds, cat two seconds). A compact heart timer shows remaining friendship; scaling remains shared with the player-relative model.

## Fireballs, denser maps and friendlier defaults

**Menu defaults:** Animals ON, Hell Sudden Death OFF, Lives OFF, Bots ON. Preferences now live under `eat-it-settings-v2`; an older `eat-it-settings` save keeps only its duration and bot difficulty so existing players pick up the new defaults. The Pluto multiplier selector and the Pluto toggle are removed: Pluto objects are always present and `matchSettings` fixes their bonus at `EAT.pluto.multiplier` (**4×**, ignoring any client value), with 8 initial, a 10-second interval and up to 14 active.

**Easier bots:** every level decides more slowly, hunts less and predicts less than before (Hard now uses 0.3 s decisions, Medium 0.58 s, Easy 0.95 s). Bots also move at 96% / 90% / 83% speed and receive 92% / 82% / 70% of normal-map growth (Hard / Medium / Easy). The growth handicap is not applied in Hell, so fireball maths stays exact. Online rooms keep using the Hard policy.

**Hell:** each match rolls **one or two** black holes. Fireballs spawn on reachable intact floor (4 at the start, then one every 3 seconds, at most 6). Touching one multiplies mass by **1.75×**; because 1.75 exceeds the 1.69× mass needed for the 1.30× radius bite rule, a single fireball lead lets you devour an otherwise equal rival from the front. Fireball growth stops at mass 1,000 (devouring still adds mass). A black hole that sweeps over a fireball swallows it and grows by 7 units of radius, capped at 150; tile destruction, bot danger and visuals all use the per-hole radius. Bots chase visible fireballs, hunt smaller players and avoid bigger ones. Fireballs render as additive glow shells with flickering flame cones and rising embers, and appear on the minimap.

**Normal maps:** City blocks are pre-filled with up to 64 buildings before loose props are placed (about 57 instead of about 36), narrow buildings pack three to a frontage row, and new Townhouse, Mansion, Office tower and Skyscraper models join Bus and Taxi. Nature adds a Farmhouse, Windmill and Giant boulder. New smaller props: hot dog, ice cream, newspaper, mailbox, parking meter, scooter, street lamp, pumpkin, carrot, beehive, campfire, scarecrow, wheelbarrow and canoe. Sky drops now come two at a time every 0.07 seconds, under a cap of 920 objects, and unclaimed drops away from players retire after 45 seconds (previously 90), so the rain stays continuous: about 900 drops per minute instead of about 350.

**Items:** at most 9 on the map, with a spawn attempt every 8 seconds. Only Speed and Magnet are placed at the start. Shield has an 8% weight, a single world item, a 45-second cooldown and lasts 15 seconds (was 30). Strike has a 40% weight, up to 3 world items and a 10-second cooldown.

## Easier devouring, big-prop respawns, longer swallows and the black-hole finale

**Devouring players:** you need **1.20×** the rival's radius (1.44× mass), down from 1.30×. The victim no longer has to fit through the opening, and inward motion is no longer checked. A bite only needs the victim's center over the **front half** of the open mouth, so dodging behind a mouth still works. Two players where one can eat the other no longer push each other apart, so the bigger one can move straight over its prey.

**Swallowing:** `beginFall` stores a rest point (`fallRestX/Y`). The prop stays where it entered and only slides in far enough for its near edge to clear the lip, instead of being pulled to the mouth's center. `fallOffset` is shared by the authority and the renderer. Props then tumble in proportion to their tip, while a symmetric drop stays level. Gravity-like acceleration carries them 320–600 units into the pit before removal, so they disappear into the darkness instead of vanishing near the rim. Durations: small props 0.95 s (was 0.48), large props 1.8 s (was 1.15), trees 2.1 s (was 1.5). Animal NPC swallows keep their 0.48 s timing.

**Big props:** creation records `bigTarget`, the number of houses, towers, vehicles and other very-large props. Every 1.2 s, up to two eaten ones respawn. 45% of them crash down from 520–760 units, and city buildings and vehicles still use their slots. Small loose props under a new footprint are cleared. Ordinary sky rain now drops 3 props per tick, up to 1,000 objects, and leaves 80 slots of headroom so big respawns always fit.

**Hell:** the floor is now 44×30 cells (3,520×2,400), and up to 9 fireballs are active (6 at the start, then one every 2.4 s). Each sweep rolls `harmless` with 50% probability. A harmless sweep moves the hole and telegraphs its path in muted lilac, but never breaks the floor; bots ignore those paths. A player who has collected **10 fireballs** devours any black hole whose center passes over their mouth, and gains +50% mass. The hole is flagged `eatenAt`/`eatenBy`, leaves `blackHoles()` (`allBlackHoles()` keeps it for the 0.8 s suck-in animation) and stops acting. The HUD shows a fireball counter toward 10, and bots with 10 fireballs intercept holes. To keep matches finite on the bigger floor, after 150 s of Hell the floor collapses inward one ring every 5 s, with a "The floor is collapsing!" notice. Eight-bot Hell phases now end in about 2–3.5 minutes.

**Menu:** every visit starts at 10 minutes with Hell OFF. Choosing 2 minutes switches Hell ON, and choosing any other duration switches it OFF; the toggle can still be changed afterwards.

`tests/eat-it-devour.test.mjs` covers these rules. The `Swallow` option in `/tests/eat-it-hell-fireballs-preview.html` shows the fall path. Online play needs the **`eat-it-match`** Edge Function redeployed with the frontend; no migration is needed.


## Too-long props wedge in the mouth

A heavy prop (`largeSwallow`, not a tree) that tips over the lip swings its full model height across the opening. If `objectHeight` is greater than `mouthOpening`, the top catches the far rim and the prop bridges the hole (`tooLongToSwallow` / `jamsInMouth` in `falling.ts`). At `jamAge` (30% of the fall), the authority converts the claim into the tree choke: the eater is locked for 3 s, the prop stays frozen at the lean where it wedged (`stuck.age`) and rides along with the mouth, then it is pushed back upright (`spit.fromTilt`) and tossed aside with no reward. It cannot be re-claimed for 1.25 s after the choke ends. A straight, centered drop (`fallTip` 0) never wedges, because the pit is deeper than every model is tall. In practice this affects skyscrapers (needs mass ≈7,800 to swallow, versus ≈1,800 to fit by area), office towers (≈3,600), windmills (≈3,100), apartments and vending machines. Bots steer around props that would wedge them. The `Jam` option in `/tests/eat-it-hell-fireballs-preview.html` shows the sequence, and `tests/eat-it-devour.test.mjs` covers it.


## Longer Strike, 1.15× bites, faster movement, zoom and clean tower falls

**Strike** now lunges **300** units (was 180) over 0.52 s (was 0.45 s); bots and the Hell safe-ground check read the same distance. **Devouring players** needs **1.15×** the rival's radius (≈1.32× mass, was 1.20×); the menu guide, danger rings and bots follow `playerEatRadiusRatio`. **Movement:** base speed 255 (was 210) and acceleration 920 (was 760), so reaching top speed takes the same time.

**Camera:** the automatic zoom keeps zooming out as you grow, down to 0.30 (was a 0.62 floor reached at mass ≈1,750), with a 0.28 curve (was 0.25), so screen radius grows as mass^0.22. Players can zoom 0.45×–2.5× on top of that with the mouse wheel or trackpad pinch, two-finger pinch on touch screens (the joystick releases while pinching), the `+`/`-` keys, or the new zoom buttons. The shadow box and camera clipping grow with the view so zoomed-out scenes keep their shadows.

**Tower falls:** tall props tipping over the lip could swing their roof past the far rim and cut through the ground (up to ~125 units on apartments, mansions and office towers that were not tall enough to wedge). `beginFall` now stores `fallLean`, the largest lean at which the roof stays inside the far rim, measured from the actual pivot, footprint and rest point; the props then sink while leaning at most that much. Props that wedge (`fallWedge`, decided at capture) tip until their front face rests on the far rim by the jam age and freeze there instead of hovering or sinking. The rest point also keeps wide footprints' side corners inside the rim where possible. Only the unavoidable corner overhang of footprints that fit by area but not by shape remains (≤ ~30 units). Online play needs **`eat-it-match`** redeployed with the frontend; no migration is needed.


## Shorter chokes, mouth-spanning wedges, Hell goal notice and the mobile HUD

**Choking** lasts `EAT.eating.chokeDuration` = **1.5 s** (was 3 s) for both oversized tree canopies and wedged tall props; re-claiming still waits 1.25 s after it ends. **Wedges** no longer freeze where the prop entered (which showed the tower from the middle of the mouth outward): the base slides onto the near rim by the jam age, pivots on it, and the tower leans across the whole opening until its front face rests on the far rim (`fallLean = acos(depth / opening)`, at least 0.25 rad).

**Hell:** for the first 5 seconds after Sudden Death begins, a centered notice says how many fireballs (`holeEatCount`, 10) devour a black hole; it hides once you have them. **Camera:** the automatic zoom-out is back to minimal (floor 0.56, curve 0.26) and manual zoom-out stops at 0.6× to keep render cost down. **Item legend** removed from the arena.

**Mobile:** touch screens get touch-specific hints ("Drag anywhere to move · Pinch to zoom"), a 7 px joystick dead zone, no long-press menu/selection/tap highlight, and Strike/Jump fire on press rather than on click. Portrait phones show status along the top (title, timer, buttons, top-3 leaderboard plus you), effects on the left under the buttons, Growth/lives/mass in the bottom-left and large round Strike/Jump buttons plus Escape in the bottom-right, all respecting safe-area insets. Landscape phones get a thinner variant. Online play needs **`eat-it-match`** redeployed with the frontend; no migration.


## Continuous tower falls, victory lap and a lighter mobile HUD

**Tower falls:** tipping props used to lean to a fixed limit (`fallLean`, about 27° for a skyscraper), freeze there, and then drop straight down. Now the lean follows one accelerating curve (`tumble`) for the whole fall. `beginFall` stores the hole along the fall axis (`fallFar`, the far rim measured from the rest point, and `fallHole`, its radius). `fallTrack` steps the fall and keeps the prop's cut through the ground plane inside the hole. When the cut would cross the far rim, the prop slides back toward the near rim. That slide only grows and eases in 0.15 of the fall early, so it happens once. The lean holds only while its cut would be wider than the whole opening (briefly, for towers barely shorter than the mouth). Footprints that fit by area but not by shape keep their overhang centered. Snapshots without `fallFar` keep the old fixed limit. `tests/eat-it-finale.test.mjs` covers this.

**Victory lap:** when the last rival is eaten (`lastStanding`), the arena freezes the result and keeps the world running on `stepEpilogue` for `EAT.match.victoryLap` = **10 s** of game time. Bots, eating and Hell continue, but nobody can be eliminated, respawn or win again. The HUD keeps the final standings. A "You win" / "{name} wins" banner counts down, then the arena fades out (`resultsFadeMs`, 0.7 s) and the results screen fades in, with Play Again and Return to Lobby. Online, the lap runs locally on a copy of the final snapshot. Other endings (size ranking, ties, solo losses) still go to results after about 1 s.

**Mobile:** on portrait phones the HUD panels are smaller (tighter padding, smaller type and icons) and more see-through (about 55% instead of 91% opacity, lighter blur), and the stacked panels sit closer to the edges. Landscape phones get the same lighter panels. Online play needs **`eat-it-match`** redeployed with the frontend for the new fall fields; no migration is needed.

## Hell growth, Strike spawns and held-item notice

**Fireballs:** each fireball now multiplies mass by **1.45×** (still above the 1.3225× needed for the 1.15× bite rule) instead of 1.75×, and the hard 1,000-mass ceiling is gone. Below `softMass` (1,200) growth is multiplicative; past it every fireball adds a fixed `softMass × 0.45` = 540 mass, so players keep growing after the 6th fireball, just more slowly.

**Strike in Hell:** Strike items no longer share the speed/jump roll. `spawnHellStrikes` drops one on reachable safe floor every `strike.hellInterval` = **4 s** while fewer than `hellMaxActive` = 3 are on the floor.
